In the Bible, Saint Paul once met a group of believers and asked them a simple question. Did you receive the Holy Spirit? And they answered: "we have never even heard that there is a Holy Spirit."

Honestly? A lot of Catholics today could say the same thing.

We know God the Father. We know Jesus. But the Holy Spirit? For most people, he is kind of a mystery. A dove on a banner. A name at the end of the Sign of the Cross.

So today, let's fix that. Who is the Holy Spirit, and what does he actually do in your life? And stay to the end, because there is a one-line prayer that the Church has prayed for centuries, and it might be the most powerful thing you learn today.

First, who is he?

The Holy Spirit is not a force. Not a feeling. Not an energy. He is a person. The third person of the Trinity. Fully God, just like the Father and the Son.

The Creed calls him "the Lord, the giver of life." And Jesus gave him a special name. He called him the Counselor. Someone who stands by your side, helps you, and speaks up for you. Jesus said: "the Counselor, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, he will teach you all things."

Now, want to see what the Holy Spirit can do to a person? Look at Saint Peter.

On the night before Jesus died, Peter was so scared that he denied even knowing Jesus. Three times. The first time, to a servant girl.

Fifty days after Easter, the apostles were gathered together. And the Book of Acts says suddenly a sound came from heaven, "like the rush of a mighty wind." Tongues as of fire rested on each of them. "And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit."

That day is called Pentecost. And watch what happens to Peter.

The same man who was too scared to admit he knew Jesus walks out into the street and preaches to a huge crowd. In public. With total courage. And the Bible says about three thousand people were baptized that day.

Same Peter. Different power. That is the Holy Spirit.

And here is the thing. That same Spirit was given to you. At Baptism, and in a special way at Confirmation.

Saint Paul says it like this: "Do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you?" A temple. God lives in you. Not just near you. In you.

So what does he actually do? The Church names two big things. Gifts and fruits.

First, the gifts. The Catechism lists seven gifts of the Holy Spirit. Think of them like tools he puts in your hands.

Wisdom, to see life the way God sees it. Understanding, to grasp what you believe. Counsel, to make good decisions. Fortitude, which means courage, to do the right thing when it's hard. Knowledge, to know God through his creation. Piety, to love God like a child loves a father. And fear of the Lord. Not being scared of God, but being amazed by him, and never wanting to lose him.

Those are the gifts. Then there are the fruits. Fruits are what grow in your life when you let the Spirit work. The Church's tradition lists twelve. Charity, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, generosity, gentleness, faithfulness, modesty, self-control, chastity.

Here is a simple test. When you see more of those in your life, the Holy Spirit is working. Just like you can tell a tree by its fruit.

And one more thing he does, maybe the most comforting of all. He helps you pray.

Ever felt like you don't know what to say to God? Saint Paul gets it. He wrote: "the Spirit helps us in our weakness; for we do not know how to pray as we ought, but the Spirit himself intercedes for us with sighs too deep for words."

So even when you have no words, he is praying in you.

Okay. The prayer I promised. It is short, and the Church has prayed it for centuries. The Catechism quotes it. "Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of your faithful and enkindle in them the fire of your love."

That's it. Before a test. Before a hard conversation. When you wake up. When you don't know what to do. Come, Holy Spirit.

So here is your challenge. Every morning this week, before you check your phone, pray those words. And then watch for the fruits. Watch for more peace, more patience, more courage.

The Holy Spirit is not forgotten by God. Don't let him be forgotten by you.
